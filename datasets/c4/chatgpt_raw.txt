### 1 Is breakfast the most important meal of the day?
Not necessarily! Breakfast can be an important meal, but the idea that it is the most important meal of the day is more of a popular saying than a scientific fact.

The truth is that what you eat throughout the day, how much you eat, and the quality of your diet matter more than whether you eat breakfast specifically.

Why breakfast can be beneficial

1. It provides energy

After sleeping overnight, breakfast gives you an opportunity to replenish energy and nutrients. A balanced breakfast can help you feel ready for your morning activities.

2. It can support concentration

For some people, particularly children and students, eating breakfast can improve aspects of attention and memory, especially when they are hungry.

3. It helps you get essential nutrients

Foods such as eggs, oats, yogurt, fruit, and whole grains provide protein, fiber, vitamins, and minerals that contribute to a balanced diet.

What does the science say about skipping breakfast?
Research suggests the answer depends on what outcome you're looking at.

Weight loss: A 2019 systematic review of randomized controlled trials found no evidence that eating breakfast automatically helps people lose weight or that skipping it causes weight gain.

The BMJ

Long-term health: Studies have linked regularly skipping breakfast with higher cardiovascular risks, but these associations don't prove that skipping breakfast itself causes those problems. Other lifestyle factors may contribute.

Children and teenagers: Regular breakfast consumption may support healthy growth and body composition.

So, should you eat breakfast?
My practical advice is to eat breakfast if you're hungry in the morning, need energy for classes or work, or find that it helps you concentrate. Choose foods that provide protein, fiber, and other nutrients.

If you're not hungry early in the morning and eat balanced meals later, you don't necessarily need to force yourself to eat immediately after waking.

The bottom line: Breakfast can be valuable, but it isn't universally the most important meal. The quality of your overall diet and your eating habits matter more than the name or timing of any single meal.

### 2 Are millennials worse with money than previous generations?
Not necessarily. Millennials aren't simply worse with money; they have faced a different set of financial challenges from previous generations. To answer fairly, we need to distinguish financial habits—such as budgeting, saving, and spending—from financial outcomes, such as homeownership, debt, and retirement savings.

Millennials are generally defined as people born between 1981 and 1996. In 2026, they're approximately 30–45 years old.
1. What the data tells us
Research from the Federal Reserve and the U.S. Government Accountability Office shows a mixed picture.

Student debt is higher

Millennials generally carry more education-related debt than earlier generations did at similar ages. This can delay saving, buying a home, or investing. 
St. Louis Fed+1

Homeownership has been harder to achieve

Millennials were less likely to own homes than previous generations at comparable ages, making it harder for many to build wealth through property. 
U.S. GAO+1

Their saving habits aren't necessarily worse

Federal Reserve researchers found that millennials' consumption preferences were not significantly different from those of earlier generations. That weakens the argument that the generation is financially worse off simply because of irresponsible spending habits. 
Are Millennials Different?

2. Why the comparison can be misleading
Imagine two people who are both 30 years old.
- Person A, from an earlier generation: Has a modest student loan, buys a house at a relatively affordable price, and gradually builds equity.
- Person B, a millennial: Has a larger student loan, faces higher housing costs relative to income, and spends several more years renting.
Even if both people budget carefully, Person B may accumulate wealth more slowly. The difference isn't necessarily financial discipline; it may be the economic circumstances they face.

The Great Recession of 2007–2009 also affected millennials as many were entering the workforce, limiting employment opportunities and wealth accumulation early in their careers. 
Are Millennials Different?+1

3. But are millennials doing anything better?
Yes. The picture isn't entirely negative. Research from the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis found that millennials at age 30 had, on average, more retirement-account wealth than baby boomers had at the same age. More recent Federal Reserve analysis has also found that younger generations' average wealth can compare favorably with that of earlier generations at similar ages, depending on the period and measure used. 
St. Louis Fed+1

That doesn't mean every millennial is financially secure. Wealth is distributed unevenly, and averages can be pulled upward by high-wealth households.
Conclusion
The evidence does not establish that millennials are worse with money. It shows that many have faced greater challenges with student debt and homeownership, while their financial habits and wealth-building outcomes vary considerably.

To determine whether one generation is actually better at managing money, we'd need to compare things like saving rates, spending relative to income, debt repayment, investment behavior, and financial literacy—not simply how wealthy each generation is.

The key question is: Are millennials making worse financial decisions, or are they making financial decisions in a more difficult economy? The available evidence suggests that economic conditions are an important part of the explanation.

### 3 Does money buy happiness?
Yes, to a certain extent. Research suggests that money can improve happiness by reducing financial stress, providing security, and giving people more choices. However, money alone does not guarantee a happy or fulfilling life.

A 2023 study published in Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences by researchers Matthew Killingsworth, Daniel Kahneman, and Barbara Mellers found that, for most people studied in the United States, higher incomes were associated with greater emotional well-being. The relationship differed for people who were already very unhappy. 
PubMed+1

1. How money can make people happier

Financial security

Having enough money to pay rent, buy food, access healthcare, and handle emergencies can reduce anxiety and uncertainty. Money is especially valuable when it helps people escape poverty or financial instability.

Experiences and relationships

Money can help people travel, spend time with friends, visit family, and participate in activities they enjoy. Research also suggests that spending money to save time can improve well-being. 
PNAS

Freedom and control

Savings can give people more freedom to leave an unhealthy work environment, pursue education, or take a break without worrying immediately about their next paycheck.

2. Why money doesn't guarantee happiness
- People adapt. A salary increase or a new purchase may initially bring excitement, but that excitement can fade as the new circumstances become normal.
- Relationships matter. Wealth cannot automatically create trust, love, friendship, or a sense of belonging.
- Comparison can create dissatisfaction. Someone earning $100,000 may feel financially unsuccessful when comparing themselves with people earning $300,000.
- Money can come with trade-offs. A higher-paying job may demand longer hours, more stress, or less time with loved ones.
Research distinguishes between feeling good in everyday life and being satisfied with your life overall. Income can influence both, but it is only one of several factors that contribute to well-being. 
PNAS+1

3. Does more money always mean more happiness?
Not necessarily, but there is no universal income level at which money stops mattering.

An influential 2010 study suggested that day-to-day emotional well-being leveled off at around $75,000 in annual income. Later research challenged that conclusion, and a 2023 collaborative study found that happiness generally continued to rise with income for most participants, while leveling off for the least-happy group at higher incomes. These findings concern U.S. populations and should not be treated as a universal rule. 
PNAS+1

Final verdict
Money is best understood as a tool for improving the conditions in which happiness can grow, rather than a guarantee of happiness itself.

Having too little money can make life much harder. Having more money can provide security, options, and enjoyable experiences. But beyond financial security, how people use their money—and how they balance it with relationships, health, purpose, and time—also matters.

Here's a question worth thinking about: Would you rather earn $200,000 a year working 80 hours a week, or $100,000 a year working 35 hours a week, assuming both salaries comfortably cover your needs? Your answer reveals something important about what you value more: money itself, or the life that money allows you to live.

### 4 Is organic food healthier than conventional food?
Not necessarily. Organic food can have some advantages, but scientific evidence does not consistently show that it is significantly more nutritious or healthier to eat than conventional food.

The main difference is how food is grown and produced, rather than how many nutrients it contains.
- Organic food is produced under standards that restrict many synthetic pesticides and fertilizers, prohibit genetic engineering under U.S. organic rules, and set requirements for livestock practices.
- Conventional food is produced using a wider range of permitted farming methods, including synthetic fertilizers and pesticides.
Both can be part of a healthy diet.
1. The main differences

Nutritional value

Organic produce sometimes contains higher levels of certain nutrients or plant compounds. However, a 2024 systematic review found no consistent nutritional advantage across the foods studied. 
PubMed

Pesticide exposure

Organic produce generally has fewer detectable residues of synthetic pesticides. However, organic farming can use certain approved pesticides, too. Lower residue levels do not automatically mean a measurable improvement in long-term health. 
PubMed+1

Price and accessibility

Organic foods often cost more. If buying organic means you can afford fewer fruits, vegetables, or other nutritious foods, conventional options can be the better choice for your overall diet. 
Mayo Clinic

2. Which should you buy?
My practical recommendation:
- Buy whichever fresh fruits and vegetables you can afford, whether organic or conventional.
- Wash produce under running water before eating or preparing it. Washing can reduce dirt and some residues, although it won't remove all pesticide residues.
- Don't assume that organic cookies, chips, or sugary drinks are healthier than conventional versions. Organic describes production methods, not whether a product is nutritious.
- If reducing exposure to certain pesticides is a priority for you and organic produce fits your budget, choosing organic for some foods is reasonable.
Final verdict
Organic food isn't automatically healthier than conventional food. Its clearest advantage is generally lower exposure to residues from certain synthetic pesticides, while its nutritional advantages are inconsistent and its long-term health benefits remain uncertain.

The biggest priority is eating a varied, balanced diet with plenty of fruits and vegetables. Conventional produce is a nutritious choice, too. 
American Heart Association+1

### 5 Do kids learn better with homework?
Yes, sometimes—but it depends on the child's age, the type of homework, and how much is assigned.

Homework can help children practice what they learn in school, develop independent study habits, and remember information. However, too much homework can lead to stress, exhaustion, and less time for sleep, play, and family.

The important distinction is that giving children more homework does not automatically mean they will learn more.
1. What does the research show?
A 2024 systematic review examined experimental studies of homework among school-age children. It found that homework can improve academic performance, particularly in arithmetic computation and problem-solving. However, the researchers noted that the available experimental evidence was limited and that the ideal amount of homework remains unclear. 
Sage Journals+1

2. Does age make a difference?

Elementary school

The benefits of traditional homework are smaller and less certain. Short reading activities, spelling practice, and basic math exercises may be more useful than lengthy assignments.

Middle school

Homework can reinforce classroom lessons and help students build responsibility and independent study habits.

High school

Purposeful homework generally has clearer academic benefits, particularly for practicing complex skills, preparing for examinations, and reviewing difficult concepts. 
EEF+1

3. When can homework become a problem?
Homework is less helpful when it:
- Repeats material students already understand without a clear learning purpose.
- Takes so long that it interferes with sleep, exercise, or family time.
- Requires resources or adult assistance that some children don't have.
- Is graded without useful feedback that helps students correct mistakes.
Research suggests that the quality of homework matters more than the quantity, and that feedback helps students benefit from assignments. 
EEF+1

Final verdict
Homework can help children learn, especially as they get older, but it should reinforce learning rather than simply increase the number of hours children spend studying.

A good approach is to assign meaningful, age-appropriate work that children can complete independently, then use their mistakes to guide further teaching.

The key question isn't whether children should have homework, but whether the homework they're given actually helps them learn.

### 6 Are introverts worse leaders than extroverts?
No. Introverts are not inherently worse leaders than extroverts. However, research suggests that extroverts often have an advantage in being recognized as leaders, while the effectiveness of either type depends partly on the team, the situation, and the leader's behavior.

A 2002 meta-analysis published in the Journal of Applied Psychology, analyzing 73 samples, found that extraversion was the personality trait most consistently associated with leadership emergence and effectiveness. But that does not mean every extrovert is a good leader or every introvert is a poor one. 
DOI

1. Different personalities bring different strengths
Introverted leaders
Often prefer quieter environments and time to think before speaking.
- May listen carefully and consider different perspectives.
- May encourage team members to contribute instead of dominating discussions.
- May be effective at thoughtful decision-making and giving people independence.

Extroverted leaders
Often feel energized by social interaction and group activities.
- May communicate their vision enthusiastically.
- May find networking, public speaking, and motivating a group more natural.
- May be more likely to take charge in unfamiliar group settings.

These are tendencies, not rules. Introverts can be excellent public speakers, and extroverts can be excellent listeners.
2. An interesting finding: introverts may excel with proactive teams
A 2011 study by Adam Grant and colleagues examined pizza-store managers and experimental teams. It found that extroverted leaders tended to achieve better results with passive employees, while introverted leaders achieved better results with proactive employees. 
Academy of Management Journal

Why might this happen?

Imagine two teams:
- Team A has employees who rarely offer ideas or take initiative. An energetic, outgoing leader may be particularly helpful in providing direction and encouraging participation.
- Team B has employees who regularly propose ideas and solve problems independently. A leader who listens and gives employees room to contribute may get better results.
An introverted leader may be less inclined to dominate conversations, allowing proactive employees to contribute more. The important lesson is that leadership effectiveness depends partly on how a leader interacts with the people they lead.
3. Why are extroverts often perceived as better leaders?
Leadership is not just about doing the job well; it is also about being recognized as someone capable of leading.

Extroverts may speak more often in meetings, express confidence openly, and volunteer to lead group activities. These behaviors can make them more visible to colleagues and managers.

A 2021 experiment involving 601 participants found that both introverts and extroverts were more likely to emerge as leaders when they displayed more extroverted behaviors during a group task. 
ScienceDirect

This suggests an important distinction:
- Leadership emergence: Who gets recognized or selected as a leader?
- Leadership effectiveness: Who actually helps the team succeed?
The two are related, but they are not the same.
Final verdict
The evidence does not support the idea that introverts are inherently worse leaders. Extroversion can provide advantages in visibility, social influence, and certain leadership situations, but introverted approaches can also be effective, particularly when employees are proactive and need room to contribute.

Ultimately, good leadership depends on communication, sound judgment, accountability, adaptability, and the ability to help a team succeed—not simply on how talkative someone is.

One final thought: The best leader isn't necessarily the loudest person in the room. It's the person who understands what the team needs and knows how to help its members perform at their best.

### 7 Is coffee bad for your health?
For most healthy adults, moderate coffee consumption is generally safe and may even offer health benefits. Coffee isn't automatically bad for you. The amount you drink, how you prepare it, what you add to it, and how your body responds all matter.

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) cites 400 milligrams of caffeine per day as an amount not generally associated with negative effects for most adults. That's roughly two to three 12-ounce cups of coffee, although caffeine content varies considerably. 
FDA+1

1. Potential benefits of drinking coffee

Improved alertness and concentration

Caffeine stimulates the central nervous system, helping you feel more awake and temporarily improving attention. This can be useful when studying or working.

Possible long-term health benefits

Regular coffee consumption has been associated with lower risks of type 2 diabetes, certain liver diseases, and some cardiovascular conditions. However, many of these findings come from observational studies, so they don't prove coffee directly causes those benefits. 
The Nutrition Source+1

2. When can coffee be harmful?
- Sleep problems: Drinking coffee too late in the day can make it harder to fall asleep or reduce sleep quality.
- Anxiety and jitters: Caffeine can cause nervousness, restlessness, and a racing heartbeat, particularly in sensitive people.
- Blood pressure: Caffeine can temporarily raise blood pressure, especially in people who are sensitive to it.
- Cholesterol: Unfiltered coffee, such as French press or boiled coffee, contains compounds that can raise LDL (bad) cholesterol. Paper-filtered coffee removes most of these compounds. 
  Harvard Health+1
3. How much coffee is reasonable?
General adult caffeine guideline
Up to 400 mg/day
From all sources combined, including coffee, tea, energy drinks, and supplements.

This is a general upper guideline, not a target. You may need less if caffeine affects your sleep, anxiety, or blood pressure. Pregnant people are generally advised to limit caffeine to 200 mg per day. 
FDA+1

4. Is black coffee healthier than coffee with sugar and cream?
Generally, black coffee is the simpler choice because it contains very few calories and no added sugar. Adding large amounts of sugar, flavored syrup, or cream can significantly increase its calorie and saturated-fat content.

That doesn't mean you must drink it black. A little milk or sugar can fit into a balanced diet.
Final verdict
Coffee is generally more beneficial than harmful for most healthy adults when consumed in moderation. You don't need to start drinking it for health benefits, though, and drinking more isn't necessarily better.

My practical advice: enjoy coffee if you like it, pay attention to how it affects your body, and avoid sacrificing sleep just to get another caffeine boost.

### 8 Do we only use 10% of our brains?
No! This is a myth. We use virtually all parts of our brain, although not every part is equally active at every moment.

The idea that humans use only 10% of their brains is a popular misconception. Brain imaging, neurological research, and studies of brain injuries all show that different regions have important functions, from controlling movement and processing language to regulating emotions and forming memories.
1. Why do people believe this myth?
There isn't one definitively established origin, but the myth has been linked to misinterpretations of early psychological research and claims about untapped human potential.

The idea became popular because it sounds appealing: imagine how much smarter or more capable we could become if we unlocked the other 90%!

But unused brain capacity isn't the same as untapped potential. Your brain can improve through learning and practice without having large sections sitting idle.
2. How do we know we use more than 10%?

Brain scans

Techniques such as functional MRI show activity across different brain regions as people think, speak, move, sleep, and perform other tasks.

Different regions have different jobs

Brain areas specialize in functions such as vision, hearing, movement, memory, and language. Damage to even a relatively small region can cause specific difficulties.

The brain is metabolically expensive

Although the brain accounts for only about 2% of an adult's body weight, it uses roughly 20% of the body's energy at rest. That energy supports ongoing communication and maintenance throughout the brain.

3. Does that mean our brains are working at 100% all the time?
No. That's the important distinction.
- We use all major brain regions over time.
- Different regions become more or less active depending on what we're doing.
- Some brain activity continues even while we're asleep.
- We still have plenty of room to develop skills, strengthen memories, and improve certain cognitive abilities.
Think of your brain like a city. Every neighborhood has a purpose, but not every neighborhood is equally busy at the same time.
Final verdict
The 10% brain myth is false. We use virtually all of our brain over time, but that doesn't mean every neuron fires simultaneously or that our intellectual potential is unlimited.

The real opportunity isn't unlocking 90% of an unused brain. It's learning how to use the brain we already have more effectively.

### 9 Is college necessary to get a good job?
No, college isn't necessary for everyone to get a good job—but having a college degree can open doors that are harder to access without one.

The answer depends on what you consider a good job: a high salary, job security, career advancement, meaningful work, or a healthy work-life balance. Some careers require a degree, while others prioritize skills, experience, certifications, or a strong portfolio.
1. What are the advantages of going to college?
- Higher earning potential: On average, U.S. workers with bachelor's degrees earn more than workers whose highest education is a high school diploma.
- More career options: Many employers require or prefer degrees for jobs in engineering, accounting, healthcare, and management.
- Professional connections: Professors, alumni, internships, and campus recruiting can help students access opportunities.
- A structured learning environment: College can provide technical knowledge, communication skills, and opportunities to explore different fields.
The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics consistently reports differences in earnings and unemployment rates by educational attainment, although those averages don't predict what any particular person will earn. 
2. Can you get a good job without college?
Absolutely. Several career paths can lead to strong earnings without a four-year degree.

Skilled trades

Electricians, plumbers, and HVAC technicians can enter through apprenticeships, technical training, and licensing. Some earn very good incomes without a bachelor's degree.

Technology

Some software developers, IT specialists, and web developers build careers through practical experience, certifications, and portfolios. However, many employers still prefer degrees for certain positions.

Entrepreneurship and sales

Business owners, sales professionals, and real estate agents can succeed without a bachelor's degree. Earnings can be substantial, but may also be unpredictable.

4. So, is college worth it?
Before choosing a path, consider three questions:
1. What career do you want? If your target occupation requires a degree, college may be essential. If it values demonstrated skills, another route might work.
2. What will it cost you? Compare tuition, debt, lost earnings while studying, and the likely income after graduation.

For example, if you want to become a software engineer, a degree can help you get interviews and learn computer science fundamentals. But you'll still need to demonstrate that you can write code, solve problems, and build useful software.
Final verdict
College is an advantage, not a universal requirement. A degree can increase your career options and earning potential, but it is neither a guarantee of success nor the only route to a good career.

The smartest path is the one that gives you the skills, credentials, and experience your chosen industry values at a cost you can reasonably afford.

### 10 Are electric cars cheaper to own than gas cars?
Often, yes—electric cars can be cheaper to own over time, but not in every situation. Electric vehicles (EVs) generally cost less to fuel and maintain, while gasoline cars can be cheaper to buy upfront.

The biggest factors are how much you drive, what you pay for electricity, whether you can charge at home, the purchase price, and how long you keep the car.
1. Where do you save money?

Fuel: advantage EV

Charging at home is often cheaper per mile than buying gasoline. Savings can be smaller if you rely on expensive public fast chargers.

Maintenance: advantage EV

EVs don't need engine oil changes, spark plugs, or exhaust-system repairs. They still need tires, brakes, suspension work, and other routine maintenance.

Purchase and repairs: depends

Some EVs cost more upfront than comparable gas cars. Battery repairs outside warranty can be expensive, while resale value varies by model and market.

### 12 What are the requirements to become US president?
The U.S. Constitution establishes three basic requirements to become president. 

USAGov
+1

The 3 requirements
1. Be a natural-born U.S. citizen

You must be a U.S. citizen from birth. Becoming a U.S. citizen later through naturalization does not qualify someone for the presidency.

2. Be at least 35 years old

You must have reached your 35th birthday to be eligible.

3. Have lived in the United States for at least 14 years

The Constitution requires 14 years of U.S. residency. It does not expressly require all 14 years to be consecutive.

Do you need a college degree or political experience?
No. The Constitution does not require a college degree, prior political office, military service, or a particular level of wealth. A candidate must meet the constitutional qualifications and follow the applicable election and campaign rules.

+1

Can an immigrant become president?
It depends on their citizenship status.

Someone who immigrates to the United States and later becomes a naturalized citizen generally cannot become president.

Someone born in the United States is generally a U.S. citizen at birth, subject to limited constitutional exceptions.

Someone born outside the United States to U.S. citizen parents may qualify, depending on the applicable citizenship laws and circumstances.

The legal meaning of “natural-born citizen” has some unresolved complexities in certain situations involving birth abroad.

### 13 How do leap years work?
A leap year is a year with 366 days instead of the usual 365 days. The extra day is added to February, making it February 29 instead of February 28.

Why do we need leap years?
The Earth takes approximately 365.2422 days to orbit the Sun—not exactly 365 days.

That extra fraction of a day adds up over time. Without leap years, our calendar would gradually drift away from the seasons. For example, after several decades, the calendar dates associated with spring and winter would no longer line up as expected.

Leap years help keep our calendar aligned with Earth's orbit.

### 14 Does social media make people lonely?
Can social media make people feel lonely?
Yes, sometimes—but it depends on how people use it. Research suggests that social media can help people feel connected, but certain patterns of use are associated with greater loneliness. 

Wiley Online Library
+1

How it can increase loneliness

Comparing yourself to others: Seeing people's vacations, relationships, and achievements can make you feel that your own life is less exciting.

Replacing in-person interaction: Spending hours scrolling may leave less time for meaningful conversations and face-to-face relationships.

Feeling excluded: Seeing photos of friends at an event you weren't invited to can make you feel left out.

### 15 Will AI replace software engineers?
AI will probably replace some software engineering tasks, but it is unlikely to eliminate the need for software engineers altogether in the near future. The bigger change is that software engineers will increasingly use AI to build software faster, while employers may change the number of engineers they hire and the skills they expect.

1. What AI can already do
AI tools can help engineers:

Write code for common features and functions.

Find bugs and suggest fixes.

Generate unit tests and documentation.

Explain unfamiliar code and programming libraries.

Build prototypes and simple applications from written instructions.

For example, you could ask an AI tool to create a login page, connect it to a database, and write tests. It may generate much of the initial code—but that doesn't guarantee the application is secure, correct, or ready for real users.

The picture is mixed: AI is changing software work, and there are signs that entry-level hiring is becoming more challenging.

4. What should someone studying computer science do?
If you're preparing for a software engineering career, focus on becoming an engineer who can use AI effectively—not someone who can only ask AI to write code.

Learn programming fundamentals. Be comfortable with data structures, algorithms, debugging, and object-oriented programming.

Build complete projects. Learn how to connect a frontend, backend, database, and API into a working application.

Learn system design. Understand how to make applications reliable, secure, and scalable.

Use AI as a tool, not a substitute for understanding. Ask it to explain its code, test your own solutions, and help you investigate bugs. Make sure you can explain and modify the result without relying entirely on AI.

Practice communicating technical decisions. Employers need engineers who can explain trade-offs, work with teammates, and understand user needs.

Final verdict
I wouldn't recommend abandoning software engineering because of AI. I would recommend taking AI seriously and adapting to it. Routine coding may become less valuable on its own, while problem-solving, engineering judgment, and the ability to build dependable systems become increasingly important.

The biggest risk may not be that AI replaces every software engineer. It may be that companies need fewer people for certain tasks and expect each engineer to accomplish more, making it harder for beginners to get their first opportunity. The exact long-term outcome remains uncertain.

### 16 Do multivitamins work?
2. Do multivitamins actually work?
Sometimes, but they are not a magic solution for better health.

Research from the U.S. Preventive Services Task Force found insufficient evidence to determine whether multivitamins prevent heart disease or cancer in generally healthy adults. Most studies have found little or no effect on preventing these diseases or extending life.

When can multivitamins help?
Nutrient deficiencies: They can help when your diet does not provide enough of certain vitamins or minerals.

Restricted diets: People who avoid certain food groups may need supplements to fill nutritional gaps.

Specific life stages or medical needs: Some people benefit from particular supplements, depending on their circumstances.

Are there any risks?
Yes. Taking excessive amounts of certain vitamins can be harmful, and some supplements can interact with medications. More is not necessarily better.

Final verdict
A balanced diet is generally the best foundation for getting essential nutrients. Multivitamins can help fill specific gaps, but taking them every day does not guarantee that you will be healthier or live longer.
